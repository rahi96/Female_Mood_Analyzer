The Radiance Engine — functional architecture, screen layouts, and feature build notes

Purpose of this document. This spec describes Beauty & Radiance — the platform’s entry wedge and its outward-facing lens on the same inner signals the other engines track. It covers the module architecture (the outer-reflects-inner thesis and the Radiance Engine), the body-image commitment that is this module’s central design discipline, the Perfect Corp AI-skin-analysis integration as the centerpiece, a proposed feature set, the AI-coach overlay, three reference screen layouts (the “Today” hero, the skin-scan result, and the inner-driver screen), a regulatory/privacy flag for counsel, and a ready-to-run Cowork prompt. Stage framing. User-facing, this stage reads: revealing how skin, energy, and outward vitality reflect inner health, turning daily signals into visible results.
0 · Data Privacy & Zero-Retention
Privacy is a first-class design principle across every Neumera module, not a compliance afterthought. The commitments below are inherited from Neumera’s core platform and privacy architecture and restated here so this document stands on its own for diligence review. They govern every feature in this specification.
Zero-retention AI
The AI keeps nothing; your account does. All AI inference runs on AWS Bedrock over private connectivity (VPC PrivateLink), under a signed Business Associate Agreement verified through AWS Artifact, with no retention of prompts or outputs by the model or the provider. The inference layer is stateless; durable value lives only in the user’s own longitudinal record. This is how zero-retention inference is reconciled with durable longitudinal storage — the record persists, the AI does not.
Trust domains and the de-identification gate
Data is separated into three trust domains with an isolated linkage store, so identity and health signal are never co-resident where the AI can see both. A fail-closed de-identification gate sits on any payload crossing from the identity zone to Bedrock: if the gate cannot strip identity, the request does not proceed.
Precision note. The platform’s privacy claims currently rest on pseudonymized status — not “de-identified” or “anonymized” — pending completion of the HIPAA §164.514(b)(1) Expert Determination scoping the AI-bound payload. Documentation and UI copy should use “pseudonymized” until that determination completes.
Regulatory posture
The FTC Health Breach Notification Rule is the primary regime at launch (no covered-entity or business-associate relationship, and no EU/UK presence at launch); HIPAA Security Rule practices, Washington’s My Health My Data Act, and GDPR readiness are maintained as the footprint expands, backed by an incident-response plan with breach-notification steps.
Commercial data boundary
The B2B provider-advertising layer targets geography and life-stage context only — never individual health signals. No module’s health data is used for ad targeting.
Highest-sensitivity data in this module
Facial biometric data from the Perfect Corp skin scan — among the most legally charged categories (Illinois BIPA and similar state laws). Handled under explicit, specific consent, with on-device processing where the SDK allows, at the highest-sensitivity tier, and never used for ad targeting (see Section 6).
1 · Architecture — the outward-facing lens
The thesis that makes this Neumera’s beauty module rather than another skincare app: outer reflects inner. Most beauty apps treat skin as a surface problem to sell product against. Neumera’s angle — straight from the stage description — is that skin, hair, and glow are readouts of sleep, stress, hydration, cycle phase, and nutrition. So this module is the presentation layer: it translates internal health into the visible outcomes people care about day to day, then reveals the inner drivers.
Structurally it is the mirror image of Cycle & Fertility. Where Cycle is the upstream keystone other engines read from, Beauty is the downstream face that reads from everything (cycle phase, sleep, stress trends). That is exactly why it is the wedge: “why is my skin breaking out / why do I look tired” is a far more immediate hook than “optimize your HRV,” and this module’s job is to convert “I want better skin” into “my skin tracks my sleep and my cycle” — the moment a user understands the whole platform.
A Radiance Engine whose pipeline is: capture (skin scan + progress photos + self-report) → correlate (against inner signals from the other engines and wearables) → reveal (the inner drivers of outer changes) → guide (modifiable levers and routine).
The central design discipline — radiance-affirming, never flaw-finding
This module’s razor’s edge is not medical, it is psychological. A skin-scoring app can easily become an appearance-anxiety engine — pathologizing normal features, ranking against an ideal, feeding body dysmorphia. The design discipline, and the module’s headline commitment: analysis frames skin around health and change-over-time (is it hydrated, is it improving), never around scoring beauty or counting flaws; no comparison to others or to an ideal, no “skin age,” no ranking; and language that never pathologizes normal skin or normal aging. This is a design constraint on every metric and every word — it is what makes the module trustworthy rather than corrosive.
The Perfect Corp integration — the centerpiece
The AI skin analysis (selfie → metrics like hydration, texture, evenness, under-eye, radiance) is the marquee feature, the hook, and the surface of the planned Perfect Corp partnership. Two build realities to design around from the start: facial images are biometric data — among the most legally charged categories (Illinois BIPA and its class-action history) — so the integration needs explicit consent, on-device processing where the SDK allows, and handling under the highest-sensitivity privacy tier; and the presentation of scan results is where the body-image discipline lives or dies. The specifics of Perfect Corp’s SDK/API and the exclusivity terms are to confirm directly in the partnership discussions; this spec addresses the integration at the architecture level, not the contract.
Proposed feature set
No feature list was specified for this module; the following mirrors the five-feature shape of the others.
AI skin analysis (Perfect Corp) — the scan and its radiance-affirming metrics.
Radiance tracking + photo journal — skin metrics and the user’s own progress photos trended over time; the “visible results” promise.
Inner-driver correlation — the differentiator: skin/glow correlated against sleep, cycle phase, stress, and hydration from the other engines.
Personalized routine guidance — routine adapted to her skin, cycle phase, and season; evidence-based (sleep, hydration, SPF), never miracle claims, never a serum-shilling engine.
Holistic radiance signals — hair, nails, energy/glow tied to inner health (nutrition, rest), framed as wellness, not diagnosis.
Stack and lighter compliance notes
Confirmed stack: Laravel API core + Python/FastAPI internal ML microservice (for the inner-outer correlation math) + Flutter client (hosting the Perfect Corp SDK). Sources: the Perfect Corp scan, user photos and self-report, and inner signals read from the other engines plus HealthKit/Health Connect and Terra. AI on AWS Bedrock, zero-retention.
Not dermatology. If the scan or the user surfaces a concerning or changing lesion, the app must not reassure and should route to a dermatologist. Melanoma is the one genuine safety item in an otherwise low-risk module.
Commercial honesty. This is where product/provider monetization is most tempting and most trust-eroding: recommendations stay genuinely personalized and disclosed, and the B2B provider-ad layer keeps to geography + life-stage only, never individual skin data, per the existing ad-layer rule.
Efficacy honesty. Radiance guidance is evidence-based (the real levers — sleep, hydration, SPF), honest about skincare’s limits, and free of miracle claims.

6 · Regulatory & privacy flag — for counsel
Beauty & Radiance is the lowest medical-risk module, but it carries a distinct and real flag set — lighter than the SaMD modules, centered on biometric privacy and body-image safety.
⚠  Facial biometric privacy, body-image safety, and the dermatology boundary
Issue. The Perfect Corp skin scan captures facial biometric data — among the most legally charged categories, with an active class-action history under Illinois’ Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA) and similar state laws. It requires explicit, specific consent; on-device processing where the SDK allows; and handling under the highest-sensitivity privacy tier.
Body-image safety. Appearance scoring can cause real psychological harm (appearance anxiety, body dysmorphia). The radiance-affirming, no-scoring, no-comparison design is not cosmetic polish — it is a first-class safety requirement and should be reviewed as such, especially given a broad female user base.
Dermatology boundary. Skin analysis is cosmetic wellness, not a medical exam. It must not diagnose skin conditions and must route concerning or changing lesions to a dermatologist (the melanoma case).
Recommended action. Have regulatory/privacy counsel review the Perfect Corp facial-biometric handling (BIPA/consent posture) and confirm the skin analysis stays framed as cosmetic wellness rather than a device claim, before this module ships. The body-image design discipline and the dermatology route-to-care should be treated as launch requirements, not enhancements.
